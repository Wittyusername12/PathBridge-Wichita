Wichita loses people at handoffs, not at the front door: only 17 emergency beds are reachable tonight

Wichita's system is fast once a person is matched. Housing takes 45 days on average, against 176 nationally 📊 community snapshot. People fall out between doors. By age 21, 42% of youth from foster care have been homeless

Wichita counted 736 people homeless on the 2025 Point-in-Time night. Of these, 195 were unsheltered, and the total was up 6.11% from 2024 📊 community snapshot.

Volume is not the core failure. The local rate of 14 per 10,000 residents sits below the national 23 per 10,000 residents. Placements average 45 days, against 176 days nationally.

The failure is at the handoffs:

  • Only 17 of 87 emergency beds are reachable tonight 📊 system status.
  • Only 38% of the 3,257 people served were housed within the year 📊 community snapshot.
  • Youth leaving foster care hit a cliff. Homelessness reaches 42% by age 21. That is above the national official poverty rate of 11.1%, which is a different measure, shown for scale.
  • Former foster youth on the KanCare extension lose coverage at 42%. That is above the national uninsured rate of 8%, also a different measure 📊 community snapshot, transition programs.
Second Light, the intended front door, still places referrals by phone because HMIS bed-matching is not live 📊 shelter inventory, system status.

Maya, age 19 and newly out of foster care, can reach CrossRoads, the city's only round-the-clock youth shelter, with 10 of 12 beds in use. Her state ID needs a birth certificate, a Social Security card and proof of residency she cannot show, which takes at least 31 days of processing. Each office visit means a $50 monthly bus pass on 30-60-minute headways with no Sunday service 📊 stabilization docs. These barriers feed each other: income poverty makes the pass unaffordable, the missing ID blocks the bank account and job that would fix the income, and the transit gap turns each 2-4-hour DCF wait into a lost day of work. If she spoke Spanish, Second Light and CrossRoads intake (English only) could not serve her, which pushes her toward 211 Kansas, Open Door, DCF or Center of Hope and adds another handoff 📊 intake network. Every move also risks her KanCare extension, which 42% of enrollees lose through notices sent to old addresses; the data holds no neighborhood income or poverty rates, so none is cited for where she lives.

An accountable handoff layer across 8 existing front doors

Relay Wichita builds no new beds. It makes the existing ones reachable and keeps people attached after each counter visit. It has four parts.

  • Bottleneck dashboard. It reads the 381 beds and 210 program slots in the records and applies each site's own entry gates. It then shows which of the 55 free beds and 76 free slots a given person can actually use. This gives Second Light the matching layer its HMIS integration has not yet delivered 📊 shelter inventory, transition programs, system status.
  • Case ownership. A navigator owns each case until the next door confirms receipt. The navigator runs a document track that starts with a City ID in 1 day. The navigator also runs a benefits-continuity checklist: SNAP's 7-day expedited processing, KanCare address updates, and DCF IL and ETV enrollment 📊 stabilization docs.
  • Landlord desk. NEXTenant partners get one named contact and a response standard. The desk pairs rapid rehousing with Housing First at HumanKind Villas 📊 rehousing options.
  • Trust and adoption reserve. The $100K reserve funds youth peer ambassadors, landlord trust-building and partner adoption.
H.O.T., Open Door, 211 Kansas, DCF IL and City ID remain the doors. Relay supplies the warm handoff that today ends at each counter.

03Delivery Model

8 front doors feeding a case owner who holds every handoff

Intake. Every person entering through any of the 8 front doors gets a Relay case record. The doors are 211 Kansas, H.O.T., Open Door, CrossRoads Youth Intake, Second Light, the Family Promise line, the DCF Wichita Service Center and Center of Hope 📊 intake network.

Fit check. The fit check applies the published prioritization table, computed in Python.

  • Transition programs are scored on years before the age limit (25), weeks to start (25), retention (25) and free slots (25).
  • Doors are scored on warm handoff (40), fit (30) and languages served (30).

Housing. Housing-ready cases go to the landlord desk. It routes them to NEXTenant partners, Hilltop Village, HumanKind Villas (Housing First) or VASH for veterans such as Robert 📊 rehousing options.

Follow-up. Checks at 30 and 90 days catch address changes before KanCare notices go astray. Worked cases on the page rank Maya's, Denise's and Robert's options under the table.

Social Return on Investment — Derivation

The analysis is a social return on investment, by the SROI Guide's (2012) method. Each outcome is valued with a financial proxy and counted from each year's people served (its rate × that year's people), over the years it lasts — fading by the drop-off of 30% a year from its second year — then adjusted for deadweight (25%), attribution (20%) and displacement (5%) and discounted at 3.5% a year (the SROI Guide's rate, HM Treasury's Green Book public-sector rate). The cost is the P&L's three years, discounted the same way. Outcomes are counted over 3 years.
