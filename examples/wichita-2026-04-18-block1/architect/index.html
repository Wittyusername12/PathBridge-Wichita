A transition program (transition programs, 8 records)
Gates: Not closed — not closed by the record's own status, the announcement or an alert; Serves this age — the person's age is inside served ages; Meets the program's own condition — requires is one of the person's conditions (no limit stated → passes); A slot free — slots available − active enrollment > 0; slots available 999 reads as no cap — assumed: a placeholder, not a count (the other programs hold 8 to 50), and the record is Medicaid coverage: "Medicaid coverage extended to age 26 for former Kansas foster youth with no income test".

Ties: Years before the age limit (the higher value first), Weeks to start (the lower value first), the record id.

  • Rent (35 points): 35 × max(0, 1 − median rent 1bd / 1000) — affordability: a lower rent is easier to keep (0: the rent is set by the voucher program)

Documents and benefits

All 10 records of stabilization docs, in unlock order: a document comes before the ones it unlocks, ties by processing days. The plan marks the ones the person needs.

  1. 1. SNAP (Food Assistance) Enrollment · benefit
    30 days to process · fee none
    Unlocks: food-security
    Known blockers: Identity and income documentation required; A phone interview is mandatory — unreliable phone access stalls applications; Homeless applicants qualify for 7-day expedited processing but are frequently not told
    Who helps: Open Door and the Kansas Food Bank operate on-site SNAP application assistance
    Expedited 7-day processing exists for homeless applicants and is consistently underused because front-line intake staff do not flag eligibility. Average single-adult benefit is roughly $234/month. Recertification falls every 6 months and is another common drop-off point.
  2. 2. KanCare (Medicaid) Enrollment · benefit
    21 days to process · fee none
    Unlocks: healthcare, mental-health-services, substance-use-treatment, prescription-medications
    Known blockers: Application requires SSN, primary identity document, and income documentation; Online portal has recurring mobile-browser stability issues; In-person at DCF runs 2-4 hours of wait; Any address change triggers automatic disenrollment requiring re-application
    Who helps: Open Door and Center of Hope staff benefits navigators who handle applications start to finish
    Aged-out foster youth qualify for KanCare extension through age 26 with no income test; general adults have income thresholds. Address instability is the leading cause of coverage loss because each move triggers re-verification and the notices go to the prior address.
  3. 3. Certified Birth Certificate · identity
    14 days to process · fee $15 · needed for Social Security Card, Kansas State ID / Driver's License
    Unlocks: state-id, social-security-card, employment, housing-application
    Known blockers: Requires knowing county and state of birth; Out-of-state orders mailed back with 2-4 week processing; Foster-care records sometimes carry name or DOB discrepancies that invalidate requests
    Who helps: DCF can order on behalf of current foster youth; Breakthrough Wichita assists former foster youth
    Kansas vital records charges $15 per copy; out-of-state runs $10-30. DCF is supposed to hand the youth a certified copy at age 18, but field data shows this happens inconsistently — many aged-out youth report never receiving one.
  4. 4. Social Security Card · identity
    10 days to process · fee none · after Certified Birth Certificate · needed for Kansas State ID / Driver's License, Bank Account Opening
    Unlocks: employment, state-id, banking, benefits-enrollment
    Known blockers: Replacement request requires a birth certificate or other primary identity document; Typical SSA office wait 2-3 hours; SSA office hours limited to weekdays 9-3
    Who helps: Breakthrough Wichita provides accompaniment and document prep for SSA visits
    Replacement is free. Local SSA office is downtown at 401 N Market St. Hard caps apply: 3 replacements per year, 10 in a lifetime. Aging-out foster youth who moved frequently often exhaust those quotas before they stabilize.
  5. 5. Kansas State ID / Driver's License · identity
    7 days to process · fee $22 · after Certified Birth Certificate, Social Security Card · needed for Bank Account Opening
    Unlocks: employment, housing-application, banking, air-travel, voting
    Known blockers: Requires a birth certificate plus Social Security card plus proof of residency; Residency requirement is the core circular barrier for anyone unsheltered; DMV wait 1-3 hours; Fee: $22 for ID, $23 for license
    Who helps: Breakthrough Wichita and Center of Hope issue residency attestation letters and can cover fees
    Kansas Real-ID compliant when issued with full documentation. The shelter-address letter is the workaround commonly used to satisfy residency, issued by Open Door or a similar intake-center partner. Some aging-out youth enter the process with zero primary documents and have to restart from the birth-certificate step.
  6. 6. School Enrollment / GED Registration · identity
    7 days to process · fee $120
    Unlocks: education-vouchers, etv-funding, employment-advancement
    Known blockers: GED test runs $120 per attempt; Identity documents required for enrollment; Testing-center scheduling is limited; Unstable housing disrupts study time and test prep
    Who helps: ETV covers GED costs for eligible foster youth; Wichita Adult Education Center runs free GED prep
    McKinney-Vento protects K-12 enrollment for homeless youth without typical documentation. That protection does not extend to GED or postsecondary enrollment. ETV covers up to $5,000/year but only with proof of active enrollment, which is the specific thing unstable housing makes hard to sustain.
  7. 7. Employment Verification Letter · housing
    5 days to process · fee none
    Unlocks: housing-application, rental-approval, transitional-housing-intake
    Known blockers: Most landlords require 30+ days of current employment; Gig work and day labor do not generate standard verification letters; Some employers delay or refuse the request
    Who helps: Breakthrough Wichita provides employment-readiness support and mediates employer communication
    Most private Wichita landlords use a 3x-rent income rule. Kansas minimum wage is $7.25/hr and typical entry-level positions run $12-15/hr. Qualifying for a market-rate 1-bedroom ($750-900/mo) requires documented income close to the top of that range, which is the inflection point where most stabilization plans stall.
  8. 8. Wichita City ID · identity
    1 day to process · fee none · needed for Bank Account Opening
    Unlocks: shelter-intake, employment, banking, library-services
    Known blockers: In-person visit to City Hall required weekdays 8-5; Applicant must prove Wichita residency, which unsheltered residents rarely can document; Photo captured on site, no remote option
    Who helps: Breakthrough Wichita provides ID-application coaching and accompaniment to City Hall
    Free municipal ID recognized by many local service providers. Not a state ID and not Real-ID compliant. Breakthrough Wichita can attest to identity in the absence of primary documents, which is how most aging-out foster youth actually obtain one.
  9. 9. Wichita Transit Bus Pass · transport
    1 day to process · fee $50
    Unlocks: employment-access, medical-appointments, benefits-offices, shelter-access
    Known blockers: Monthly pass costs $50 which is prohibitive for a zero-income client; Purchase channels limited to the transit center or online; Reduced fare requires a separate application and supporting documentation
    Who helps: Center of Hope, Salvation Army, and Open Door distribute limited monthly pass allotments
    Wichita Transit runs 30-60 minute headways and no Sunday service. Shelter and intake sites cluster along the N Market corridor, but the employment and affordable-housing stock is spread across the metro — making transit the dominant constraint on stabilization for anyone without a car.
  10. 10. Bank Account Opening · housing
    1 day to process · fee none · after Wichita City ID, Social Security Card, Kansas State ID / Driver's License
    Unlocks: direct-deposit-employment, housing-rent-payments, benefits-direct-deposit
    Known blockers: A state-issued ID or two alternate IDs are required; Prior overdraft history in ChexSystems blocks mainstream bank accounts; Some banks enforce minimum-balance requirements
    Who helps: Breakthrough Wichita partners with credit unions offering second-chance accounts that skip ChexSystems
    Second-chance banking exists through Intrust Bank and KANZA Cooperative programs for people exiting homelessness. Without any account, the alternative is check-cashing storefronts that cut 2-5% off every paycheck and disbursement.

All 8 programs (transition programs records), with their age windows. With a person chosen, the programs they are inside come first, the one whose window ends soonest at the top (the youth cliff), with the years left and the person's gate result.

  1. DCF Independent Living Program · ages 16-21 · intake lag 0 weeks · 16 free
    Eligibility: Current or former foster youth whose case was open after age 16
  2. Education & Training Voucher (ETV) · ages 16-23 · intake lag 4 weeks · 22 free
    Eligibility: Foster youth actively enrolled in postsecondary education or vocational training
  3. Aged-Out Medical (KanCare Extension) · ages 18-26 · intake lag 2 weeks · no cap (slots available 999 in the data)
    Eligibility: Former foster youth who aged out of care while in Kansas custody
  4. SOUL Family · ages 16-26 · intake lag 1 week · 13 free
    Eligibility: Youth aging out of foster care or formerly in foster care seeking permanent connections
  5. TRAIL (Transitioning Resources for Advancing Independent Living) · ages 16-21 · intake lag 3 weeks · 10 free
    Eligibility: Foster youth preparing for independence or recently aged out
  6. CrossRoads Emergency Youth Shelter · ages 18-24 · intake lag 0 weeks · 2 free
    Eligibility: Homeless or at-risk youth, including foster youth in acute crisis
  7. BRIDGES Transitional Housing · ages 16-22 · intake lag 6 weeks · 1 free
    Eligibility: Youth aging out of foster care or homeless youth with an active case plan
  8. EmberHope Connections · ages 12-21 · intake lag 2 weeks · 12 free
    Eligibility: Foster youth, at-risk youth, and families in active crisis

🤖 How This Tool Was Built — AI Process

Who this serves: People looking for homelessness and youth transitions services, the case workers who refer them, and the planners who track where services fall short.

Built by a Python harness that runs fixed rules. The Get Help tab ranks in your browser with the engine's published table (its own copy of the engine's rules, the same rules as the Python engine, which the tests compare); one Claude call at build time (claude-opus-5-5) wrote the “Why this order” notes from the engine's figures; Python keeps a sentence only if its numbers are among that plan's figures and it names no option outside that plan, and an option no kept sentence names gets the engine's own line (its rank, score and points), marked as the engine's. The engine decides the order, never the model. Claude helped write and test the harness and this template.

1. Data Ingestion — 7 challenge API endpoints fetched at run time: records with a name or an address form one resource index, alert lists become events, endpoints this harness knows by name feed the dashboard’s figures and events, and any other endpoint is counted; field names are matched by keyword rules.

2. Domain Label — “Homelessness and Youth Transitions” is the Oracle track's (Claude's) reading of the same data; this harness's keyword rules choose the charted indicators.

3. Severity — this data has no per-area figures, so the dashboard shows its system-wide figures as given.

4. Rendering — one HTML page with the data embedded as JSON, charts drawn by Chart.js (inlined, so they need no network), and views that filter each other.

5. Curveball — at the curveball this harness reads the change list Python computes (every endpoint compared with this run's snapshot), marks a resource closed when the announcement names it as closed in the same sentence, and lists what changed; a record whose own status reads closed is marked closed by the data in both blocks.

6. Ask AI — the chat panel sends your question and this page's data summary to Claude through a server-side proxy; no key is on the page. Try: “Which Rehousing Options resources are open?”
